BUG: Robot forgets where the pill bottle is after the base turns

Description: When the robot rotates to search, it loses the bottle location it already found, so it has to search again. The cause is that the bottle position was kept relative to the robot/camera, which changes every time the base moves. It also spins the whole base to search even though the head camera can turn on its own.

Fix: New ROS 2 package bottle_tracking. bottle_memory saves the bottle in the odom frame, which stays fixed to the room, so the position is still right after turning. head_tracker keeps the head pointed at the saved position while the base moves. head_search sweeps the head first and only rotates the base if the head can't find the bottle. approach_planner moves the base once so the arm ends up in front of the bottle. The existing detector only needs to publish its detections on /pill_bottle/detection (see detection_publisher.py).

Tasks: Add the bottle_tracking package to the workspace. Launch stretch_driver with broadcast_odom_tf:=True. Publish detections from the perception code. Replace the old spin-to-search code in the state machine with calls to /head_search/search and /approach_planner/execute. Calibrate standoff_m and arm_x_offset_m. Check pan_sign and tilt_sign on the robot.

Acceptance criteria: T3, T5, T12, T15a and T15b pass.

Labels: bug, top cam, robot movement, search

T1a: Planning test: create and print a grasp plan from known bottle coordinates (no motion)

Description: Test the planner by itself. The bottle coordinates are typed in directly, with no perception, and the robot does not move. The tester prints the full plan so we can read what the robot would do, then checks it against the criteria below. This separates bad-plan failures from bad-execution failures.

Printed plan format: The tester prints the input bottle pose, then every step in order with the joint or base command and its target value, then the predicted final gripper position and clearance. Example:
PLAN for bottle at x=0.15 y=-0.62 z=0.76 (base_link), upright
Step 1  STOW        lift=0.85 m  arm=0.00 m  wrist_yaw=0.00 rad  gripper=open
Step 2  BASE_ROTATE +8.3 deg (line the arm up with the bottle)
Step 3  BASE_MOVE   +0.15 m forward
Step 4  LIFT        0.85 -> 0.80 m (gripper at bottle mid-height + 4 cm)
Step 5  ARM_EXTEND  0.00 -> 0.41 m (stop 3 cm before the bottle)
Step 6  LIFT        0.80 -> 0.76 m (gripper at bottle mid-height)
Step 7  ARM_EXTEND  0.41 -> 0.45 m (fingers around the bottle)
Step 8  GRIPPER     close
Step 9  LIFT        0.76 -> 0.86 m (lift 10 cm)
Predicted grasp point: 0.4 cm from bottle center height, min table clearance 4.0 cm
If no plan exists, it prints NO_PLAN and the reason, for example "bottle 1.1 m away, max arm reach 0.52 m".

Setup: Bottle pose given as x, y, z in base_link plus orientation (upright or lying down). The planner runs in dry-run mode: it builds and prints the plan, but no motion commands are sent to the robot.

Conditions: 5 reachable poses, upright (add lying down once that grasp strategy exists), and 2 unreachable poses (too far away, below table height). 10 trials each.

Success for a reachable pose: A plan is returned within 5 seconds and printed. The gripper stays at least 3 cm above the table until the final approach. The final grasp point is within 1 cm of the bottle's center height, so the middle of the fingers holds the bottle. All joint targets are within their limits.

Success for an unreachable pose: The planner returns NO_PLAN with a reason, does not crash and sends no motion command.

Outcome codes: SUCCESS, NO_PLAN (a fail on a reachable pose), COLLISION (contact predicted), TIMEOUT, ERROR.

Metrics to log: plan_time_s, num_steps, min_table_clearance_cm, grasp_height_offset_cm, plus the full printed plan saved in notes.

Pass criteria: At least 95% success on reachable poses, and 100% correct NO_PLAN on unreachable poses.

How to run: python3 testing/run_manual_test.py T1a (until the grasp planner has its own tester).

Labels: component testing, planning, robot movement

T3: Remember and track the bottle while the base moves

Description: Right now the robot forgets where the bottle is after it turns. Once the bottle is detected, its position is saved in the odom (world) frame, which does not move when the robot does. While the base rotates or drives, the head keeps pointing at the saved position by changing its pan and tilt to cancel out the base motion. If the head can't keep the bottle in view, for example because the head hits its pan limit, the robot still keeps the saved position and looks straight back at it after the motion finishes. After the motion, the robot re-detects the bottle and updates the saved position if the new detection is within 10 cm of the old one. If the bottle is more than 10 cm from where it was saved, the robot treats it as moved (see T12).

Note: This is more of an integration test than a pure component test, since it uses perception, TF, base motion and the head together.

Part A, tracking during motion: The bottle is detected and centered in the head camera, then the base rotates by +30, -30, +90, -90 and 180 degrees (10 trials each), and also drives forward 0.3 m and backward 0.3 m (10 trials each). Success when the bottle stays in the camera view for at least 90% of the motion (when the head's pan range allows it), stays within 10 degrees of image center during the motion, and is within 5 degrees of image center within 2 seconds of the motion stopping.

Part B, memory without tracking: The same rotations, but head tracking is turned off, so the head is frozen during the turn. Success when, after the turn, the head points straight at the saved position within 3 seconds without re-scanning, the bottle is within 5 degrees of image center, and the saved position and the re-detected position agree within 3 cm.

Outcome codes: SUCCESS, MISS (the head points at the wrong place), NOT_FOUND, TIMEOUT, ERROR.

Metrics to log: motion_type, rotation_deg, percent_frames_in_view, max_tracking_error_deg, reacquire_time_s, position_drift_cm.

Pass criteria: At least 90% for Part A and at least 90% for Part B. The mean position_drift_cm should stay under 3 cm. If drift grows with the size of the rotation, the odometry or TF needs tuning.

How to run: ros2 run bottle_tracking t3_tracking_tester

Labels: integration test, perception, robot movement, top cam

T5: Search with the head first, rotating the base only if needed

Description: The robot should not spin the whole base every time it searches. The order is: (1) Check the saved bottle position from T3, and if there is one, look there first. (2) If not, sweep the head camera through its full pan and tilt range while the base stays still. (3) Only if the head sweep finds nothing, rotate the base in steps of about 90 degrees and sweep the head again at each step. The test ends when the bottle is found and its position is saved. Moving to the bottle afterward is tested in T15a and T15b.

Conditions: Bottle in view of the head sweep but not straight ahead (left, right, near the edge of the pan range): 10 trials each. Bottle behind the robot, outside the head's range: 10 trials. Bottle far away but in view (2 to 3 m): 10 trials.

Success for each trial: If the bottle is within the head's range, it is found with zero base rotation. If it is outside the head's range, it is found with at most one full base rotation. The bottle is found within 60 seconds, and its saved position is within 3 cm of the measured position (within 5 cm at 2 to 3 m).

Outcome codes: SUCCESS, NOT_FOUND, MISS (spun the base when the head alone could have found the bottle, or the position error was too large), FALSE_POSITIVE, TIMEOUT, ERROR.

Metrics to log: search_time_s, base_rotation_deg_during_search, num_base_rotations, error_cm.

Pass criteria: At least 90% overall, and 100% zero-base-rotation searches when the bottle is within the head's range.

How to run: ros2 run bottle_tracking t5_search_tester

T15a: Approach planning test: plan the base motion to a far-away bottle (no motion)

Description: Test the approach planner by itself. The bottle's position is typed in directly (no perception), and the robot does not move. The planner works out where the base needs to end up so the arm is lined up with the bottle and the bottle is within arm reach, then prints every base motion needed to get there. The Stretch arm extends out the side of the base, so the final pose has the bottle beside the robot, not in front of it. This test checks the plan only. T15b checks driving it.

Printed plan format: The tester prints the bottle's position, the target base pose, every step in order, and the predicted final alignment. Example:
APPROACH PLAN for bottle at x=1.40 y=0.35 z=0.76 (odom), robot at x=0.00 y=0.00 heading=0.0 deg
Target base pose: x=0.91 y=0.23 heading=104.0 deg (bottle 0.50 m out the arm side)
Step 1  BASE_ROTATE  +14.0 deg (face the target base pose)
Step 2  BASE_DRIVE   +0.94 m (drive straight)
Step 3  BASE_ROTATE  +90.0 deg (turn so the arm side faces the bottle)
Step 4  HEAD_TRACK   keep the bottle centered during the steps above
Predicted final alignment: bottle 0.50 m from base along the arm line, 0.0 cm off the arm line, 10 cm table clearance
If no plan exists, it prints NO_PLAN and the reason, for example "bottle 4.20 m away, max approach distance 3.50 m" or "bottle height 1.35 m outside lift range 0.05-1.05 m".

Setup: The bottle position is given in the odom frame, with the robot at x=0, y=0, heading 0. The planner runs in dry-run mode: it builds and prints the plan, but no base commands are sent to the robot. No robot is needed for this test.

Conditions: Bottle ahead of the robot at 1.0, 2.0 and 3.0 m. Bottle to the left and to the right at 1.5 m. Bottle behind the robot at 1.5 m. Bottle already within reach (no motion needed). 10 trials each, with about 1 cm of noise added to the bottle position each trial. Plus 2 cases that should fail: the bottle is too far away (beyond the max approach distance), and the bottle is above the lift range.

Success for a reachable bottle: A plan is returned within 2 seconds and printed. The predicted final pose puts the bottle within the arm's reach and within 2 cm of the arm's line of extension. The predicted clearance between the base and the table edge is at least 10 cm. The plan uses at most 2 rotations and 1 straight drive (no extra spinning). If the bottle is already within reach, the plan has no drive step.

Success for an unreachable bottle: The planner returns NO_PLAN with a reason, does not crash and sends no motion command.

Outcome codes: SUCCESS, NO_PLAN (a fail on a reachable bottle), MISS (the predicted final pose is not lined up or is out of reach), COLLISION (the predicted clearance is under 10 cm), TIMEOUT, ERROR.

Metrics to log: plan_time_s, start_distance_m, total_rotation_deg, total_drive_m, num_steps, predicted_align_error_cm, predicted_table_clearance_cm, plus the full printed plan saved in notes.

Pass criteria: At least 95% on reachable bottles, and 100% correct NO_PLAN on unreachable bottles.

How to run: ros2 run bottle_tracking t15a_approach_plan_tester

T15b: Approach motion test: drive the base to the bottle using the approach plan

Description: Same kind of known bottle positions as T15a, but now the robot drives the plan. The plan is made from the tape-measured position, not perception. The plan prints before it runs (same format as T15a), so a failed trial can be traced to a specific step. While the base moves, the head keeps tracking the bottle (T3).

Setup: The robot starts on a taped start mark and the bottle is on a taped mark with a measured position. That position goes straight to the approach planner, then the base executes the plan.

Conditions: Bottle ahead at 1.0, 2.0 and 3.0 m. Bottle to the left and to the right at 1.5 m. Bottle behind at 1.5 m. 10 trials each.

Success for each trial: The base finishes every step without touching the table or knocking the bottle. At the end, the bottle is within arm reach and within 3 cm of the arm's line of extension (measured with a tape measure). The base heading is within 3 degrees of the planned final heading. The base stops at least 10 cm from the table edge. The bottle stayed in the camera view for at least 90% of the motion. The whole approach takes 45 seconds or less. The robot ends in a pose where T1b can start.

Outcome codes: SUCCESS, MISS (stopped out of reach or not lined up), COLLISION, TIMEOUT, ERROR.

Metrics to log: exec_time_s, start_distance_m, final_align_error_cm, final_heading_error_deg, final_reach_m, table_clearance_cm, percent_frames_in_view, failed_step (the step number from the printed plan).

Pass criteria: At least 90% overall. If the alignment error grows with distance, the problem is odometry drift. The approach_planner node fixes this with one small forward/back correction after a fresh detection once the robot is close.

How to run: ros2 run bottle_tracking t15b_approach_motion_tester --ros-args --params-file <workspace>/src/bottle_tracking/config/params.yaml
